The Method

The 4 Rules — and the real reason they work

1
If you use it, put it back.
Every single time. Without exception.
This is the rule that stops clutter before it starts. The counter doesn't get buried because of one bad night — it gets buried because of 47 small decisions to "deal with it later." When every item goes back to its home immediately after use, there's nothing to reset at the end of the day.
The 2026 trend in organizing is "micro-habit systems" — tiny actions that happen automatically rather than requiring willpower. This rule is exactly that.
2
If you open it, close it.
Drawers, cabinets, doors. Order is also something you see.
Half-open cabinets and drawers make a tidy space look chaotic. Your brain reads "open" as "unfinished." Closing everything you open is a 2-second habit that changes how a room feels — without moving a single item. It's the fastest visual reset that exists.
Professional organizers call this "visual calm." Research shows that visual clutter increases cortisol levels — the stress hormone. Open drawers count as visual clutter.
3
If you make a mess, clean it now.
Not later. Now. Later always turns into never.
"Later" is the most expensive word in home organization. Crumbs become crusted. Dishes become a pile. One unwashed pot becomes a full sink that nobody wants to touch. The time it takes to clean something immediately is always less than the time it takes to clean it after it's sat for hours — or days.
This is the core of what organizers call "the avalanche effect." Clutter multiplies because messy surfaces give permission for more mess. One clean surface stays clean. One messy surface collects everything.
4
If it doesn't have a home, give it one — or let it go.
Homeless items are the root of all chaos.
Every item sitting on your counter, floor, or that chair in the bedroom is there because it doesn't have a designated place. That's not a cleaning problem — it's a systems problem. Give it a permanent home and it will find its way there automatically. If you can't find a logical home for it, that's a sign you may not need it.
In every home I've organized in Denver, the #1 source of chronic clutter is always items without a home. Not too little storage. Not too little time. Items that were never assigned a place.
Room by Room

Apply the 4 rules in every space

🍳
Kitchen
  • Counters cleared immediately after cooking
  • Cabinets and drawers always closed
  • Dishes washed or in dishwasher same night
  • Every appliance has a designated spot
🚪
Entryway
  • Bags go on hooks — never on the floor
  • Shoes in their spot the moment you take them off
  • Mail sorted the day it arrives (trash or file)
  • Nothing lives on the floor long-term
🛏
Bedroom
  • Clothes go in hamper or back in closet — not the chair
  • Nightstand surfaces cleared each morning
  • Closet door stays closed (visual calm)
  • Nothing stored under the bed without a bin and a reason
🛋
Living Areas
  • Blankets folded and returned after use
  • Remotes, books, toys back in their spot daily
  • Surfaces wiped and reset before bed
  • Ask: does this item actually belong in this room?
🚿
Bathroom
  • Counter cleared after every routine
  • Products have assigned spots in drawers
  • Towels hung immediately after use
  • Weekly: check for expired or unused products
🎒
Kids' Spaces
  • Bins at their eye level with picture labels
  • One toy category out at a time
  • Backpacks have a hook — always the same hook
  • Weekly edit: 1 item out for every new item in
Your Daily Reset

5 minutes a day keeps the chaos away

Morning — 5 min

Make the bed immediately after waking
Clear kitchen counters from overnight
Close every open drawer and cabinet
Quick scan of entryway — nothing on floor
Any homeless items from yesterday — assign a home now

Evening — 10 min

Kitchen reset: counters, dishwasher running
Walk through main areas — put back anything out of place
Clothes in hamper or closet — not the chair
Tomorrow prep: bags, clothes, breakfast thought through
Surfaces in common areas cleared before bed
