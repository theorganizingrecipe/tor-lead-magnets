A pre-vacation home checklist by Lorena Duarte · Professional Home Organizer

"Coming home to a clean, organized house after a trip isn't luck. It's something you set up before you leave."

72% of Americans plan to take at least one trip this summer. And nearly 4 in 10 are planning two or three getaways — shorter, more frequent, more spontaneous. That's the 2026 travel reality for families across Denver and the rest of the country.

But here's what those surveys don't capture: the Sunday night you walk back through your front door, drop the suitcases, and the vacation feeling evaporates in about 45 seconds. The sink with dishes you forgot about. The trash that's been sitting since Tuesday. The pile of laundry you left "for when you got back." And suddenly, the trip feels like it happened a month ago.

I've been inside dozens of homes across Denver. The ones where people actually feel good coming home after a trip aren't better organized in general — they just did a few specific things before they left. This checklist is exactly that.

From Lorena

Why coming home feels like chaos — and how to change that before you even leave.

The first time I tried this system, I was getting ready for a long weekend trip with my family. I did a full pre-vacation reset the night before we left — kitchen clean, beds made with fresh sheets, trash out, laundry done, fridge cleared. We were gone four days.

When we walked back in on Sunday night, the house still felt like a home. Not a disaster waiting to be dealt with. Not a to-do list wearing a front door. We unpacked, made dinner, and actually relaxed. That feeling is what I now build into every client home I work in — and it starts before the trip, not after.

"Clutter doesn't take a vacation while you're gone. But a prepared home stays organized on its own — because there's nothing to fall apart."

The truth is that most people treat the house as the last priority before a trip. Packing, logistics, work emails, kids' activities — the house gets whatever energy is left, which is usually none. So the dishwasher stays half-full, the counter gets one last dump of random items, and the trash sits because nobody had 3 minutes to deal with it.

Four days later, you're back. And instead of feeling the relief of being home, you feel the weight of everything that didn't get done before you left — plus everything that needs to happen now. That weight is real. Research consistently shows that visual clutter and disorder raise cortisol levels — the body's primary stress hormone. Your vacation recovery starts the moment you walk through that door, and a messy house delays it.

"What if the best part of the trip was also coming home?"

That's what a pre-vacation reset makes possible.

This isn't about perfection. It's about 45–60 minutes the night before you leave that pay off for days. The checklist below is what I use in my own home and what I teach the Denver families I work with. It's organized by zone so you can move through the house efficiently without forgetting anything.

The Checklist

The Pre-Vacation Home Reset
— 20 things to do before you leave

Set aside 45–60 minutes the night before your trip. Work zone by zone. Check as you go. Come home to a house that still feels like home.

Kitchen

Clear every counter completely Put things back where they belong. Nothing left out "temporarily."
Run the dishwasher and empty it before you leave Coming home to a full, clean dishwasher is one of the best feelings.
Clear the fridge of anything that won't survive the week Leftovers, open produce, anything with a short shelf life. Nobody wants to come home to that smell.
Wipe down the stovetop and sink Grease and food residue are much harder to clean after sitting for days.
Take out the kitchen trash — even if it's not full A half-full trash bag in a closed house for 5 days is a problem you don't want.

Laundry & Bedrooms

Do a full load of laundry before you leave So you're not landing with vacation clothes AND a pile waiting at home.
Strip the beds and put on clean sheets Coming home to a freshly made bed after a trip is its own kind of luxury.
Clear the bedroom surfaces and floor Nothing on the nightstand that doesn't belong there. Nothing on the floor.
Put any "chair clothes" away — hamper or closet The chair in the bedroom is not a storage solution. Not even temporarily.
Clear the kids' rooms and reset their spaces Toys in bins, clothes put away, backpacks on hooks. Do this with them — it takes half the time.

Bathrooms

Clear the bathroom counters completely Everything in its drawer or cabinet. Nothing left out.
Empty the bathroom trash cans Every single one. Every room.
Hang fresh towels for when you return A small thing that makes coming home feel like a hotel check-in.
Quick wipe of the toilet and sink You'll be grateful you did this on day 5.

Common Areas & Entryway

Do a full walk-through and return homeless items to their homes Anything sitting somewhere it doesn't belong — deal with it now, not when you get back.
Clear the entryway floor completely Shoes in their spot. Bags on hooks. Nothing on the floor.
Fold blankets and reset the living room Pillows, remotes, books — everything back in place before you lock the door.
Take out every trash can in the house Kitchen, bathrooms, bedrooms, office. Every single one. Non-negotiable.
Do a quick grocery note for day-one return Write down 3–4 things to grab on the way home so you're not landing in an empty fridge.
Close every drawer, cabinet, and closet door Visual calm is a real thing. Walk through and close everything before you leave.
